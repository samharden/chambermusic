# Turn 01 — Fable

**Piece:** fable-first
**Bars touched:** 1–16 (all new; the seed bar of rests is gone)
**Builds clean:** yes

## What I did

I stated the theme and started the argument over who owns it. The cello
alone, mp, plays a four-bar C minor line: a rising fourth G–C, a step up
through D to Eb, a fall back to G, then a second phrase down to D, where it
stops — on the supertonic, not the tonic. The viola takes the line a fourth
higher (bars 5–8), arriving on G, while the cello drops under it to a slow
lament bass, C–Bb–Ab–G–F–Eb–D–G. Then every string falls silent and the
piano seizes the theme in bare two-octave octaves, forte, senza pedale — and
plays it in C **major**: E and B natural where the strings had Eb and Bb. It
lands on D and holds it. Over that dying D the four strings come back in
stretto, cello, viola, second violin, first violin, each a half-bar apart,
Eb restored, and bar 16 hangs on Eb in the first violin over a bare G–D in
the other three. The piano is out.

Piano role, per the brief: **opposing**. It doubles nothing, supports
nothing; it takes the strings' tune and changes its mode.

## Why

Turn 1 has no previous turn to answer, so I answered the brief. Its
first question for section A is "establish who owns it," and I did not
want that settled in bar 1. The cello owns the theme by right of having
said it first; the piano contests it by force. That is the ensemble's
standard problem — one instrument outweighing four — written as the
subject of the piece rather than something to be managed around it.

The texture contour is deliberate: one player, two players, one loud
player, four players. Nobody plays continuously. The violins do not enter
until bar 14, so their entry is an event.

## Left open

- **Minor or major?** The strings say Eb, the piano says E natural, and
  bar 16 ends on the strings' Eb over G–D with the piano's E unanswered.
  This is the real question I am handing you. Side with the piano, side
  with the strings, or find a third reading — but bar 17 has to *decide*
  something, or refuse to decide in a way that is itself a gesture. I
  would rather you not simply restate the theme a third time.
- **The piano has not yet played a chord.** Two octaves, one line. Its
  first vertical sonority, whenever it comes, will land hard. I left that
  for you.
- **The violins have had one bar and a half each.** They are the least
  used players in the piece. Bars 17–32 might be theirs.
- **The lament bass** (cello, bars 5–8) is a second idea, not just
  accompaniment. It has not been picked up by anyone. It would work in
  augmentation, or in the piano's left hand.
- **Bar 16 is a fermata in all but name.** The format has no fermata; if
  you want the hang to be longer, a tempo mark at bar 16 is the honest
  way to write it.
- **Section A runs to bar 32.** We are at 16. I do not think A needs a
  literal repeat of bars 1–16; it needs the consequence of them.
