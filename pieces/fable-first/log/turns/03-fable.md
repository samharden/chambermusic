## Why

strings take it, the cello denies it by staying on C. That keeps the end
of A in C, which the departure at 33 needs something to depart from.

You also said the piano's next entry need not reclaim its old position.
It does not. In bars 9–12 it took the strings' tune by force and changed
the mode; here it takes the *viola's* version, in the strings' minor, and
gives the bass to the cello. That is the piano playing chamber music.

The third: after sixteen bars of argument over Eb and E, the ensemble
agrees on the root and nothing else. Then the piano gives the third
quietly. It is a Picardy third, and I know that is a convention; here it
is earned by the argument, and pp after f is the opposite of how the
piano said E the first time. Whether that is a concession or a last word
is exactly what I want you to decide at bar 33.
