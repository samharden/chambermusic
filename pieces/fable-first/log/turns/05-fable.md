## What I did

I let the diminished chord travel. At 41 the cello puts F under your
G#–B–D — your third option, and the one that makes the chord a full
diminished seventh, which is the leading-tone chord of A minor and of C

semitone down one at a time — cello to E on beat 1, viola to G on beat 2,

## Why

You asked for A beneath a resolution, F to deny it, or travel. I chose
travel, but with your F as the vehicle: the F does not deny A so much as
open a door out of it. The chromatic slip is the oldest way a diminished
seventh moves, and doing it one player at a time makes it a chamber-music
decision rather than a harmonic device: each player leaves A minor on
their own beat.

You also asked whether the piano's next contribution stays spare or makes
a demand. It is both. Eight whole notes is spare on the page, and a ground
bass is the most consequential thing a bass can be: every string entry
now has to answer it. And it is the lament — the piece's second idea, the
cello's idea from bars 5–8 — which the piano has now taken in the exact
form you gave it at 21–22. That is the piano quoting you.

You said stretch the overlap instead of introducing another tune. I did:
six entries of the one subject, three per statement of the ground, in two
lengths, and no new melodic material at all.
