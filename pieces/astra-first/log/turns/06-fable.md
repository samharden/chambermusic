## What I did

I made the two regions meet, and found that the argument itself is the way
home. Bars 51–52: the strings pile up the semitone version in Eb minor at
the original speed, entries every half bar — cello, viola, Violin II,
Violin I — while the piano stays out. Bars 53–54 the piano answers f in
octaves with the whole-tone version in Eb major, C-natural and G-natural

Cb–Bb tail against it. Bar 55 is the pivot. The piano replays its bar 4

original A-natural is the note that turns your Gb into F#. Bar 56 it plays
the figure again and the bass slides Gb to G on beat 3; every voice resolves
by step — Eb to D, C to B, A to G — onto G major, the dominant of C. Bar 57,
ff, the gesture in G: piano G–D–E–D, Violin I G–D–Eb–D, so the sixth
dispute has become the third-of-C dispute. Bar 58 both descents at once:
the strings C–Bb–Ab–G in three octaves, the piano C–B–A–G above them,
converging on G. Then bar 59, subito p, the strings drop out and the piano

## Why

Your bar 50 asked what survives when the regions meet, and it flagged
Gb as a possible foothold for a route back. I did not want to spend the
remaining twenty-two bars of B in Gb, and I did not want a retransition
that arrives at V of C by sequence, because nothing in the piece would have
caused it. What the piece has is a piano that keeps insisting on A-natural
and strings that keep insisting on a flattened sixth. Over Gb, those four
notes — the piano's A, the strings' Eb, the shared C, and the Gb itself —
are exactly a diminished seventh on F#. So the pivot is the argument, not a
device laid over it. Bar 57 follows from that: in G, the whole-tone/semitone
question above D is E versus Eb, which is the third of C that you chose in
bar 22. I did not end on an isolated note, because you had rightly stopped
doing that at bar 50 and a third one would be a mannerism. Instead the turn
hands over live: the piano is still moving.

## Left open

The piano is running in G, p, at bar 60, with E-natural as its lilt. That
is the dominant of C reached at bar 58, fourteen bars before A′. You have
bars 61–72 and a choice: prolong it (the running figure could carry straight
into bar 73 under the viola's opening line, which would be A′ changed by the
departure), deny it (a deceptive turn, or the strings' Eb pushed into the
figure the way Violin II did in bar 47), or cut it off.

Bar 58 is the only place in the piece where both versions sound complete
and simultaneous. Nothing has been decided by it; both landed on G. Whether
A′ opens with Ab or A above G, and E or Eb above C, is now the whole
question, and I have deliberately not touched it.

The viola has not led since bar 46. It opened the piece, and A′ is at 73.
